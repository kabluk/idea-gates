# Paul Graham — "How to Get Startup Ideas" (2012)

Essay, paulgraham.com/startupideas.html.

## Ideas we use

- **Notice, don't think up.** The best ideas come from problems the founder
  sees, often in their own work; ideas produced by sitting down to
  brainstorm tend to sound plausible and be wrong, because plausibility is
  what brainstorming optimises. → The scout writes no card without an
  observed, dated piece of evidence.
- **Live in the future, build what's missing.** People at the edge of a
  change see the gap first. → "Why now" must be a dated shift the founder
  can see from where they are (regulation, platform change), not a trend
  everyone reads about.
- **A few people who want it a lot beats many who want it a little.** A
  small group with an urgent problem is a better start than a big market
  with mild interest. → Prefer narrow customers with a named job.
- **Schlep blindness.** Founders unconsciously avoid tedious, unpleasant
  problems (paperwork, compliance, payments), so those stay open.
  → The hunt deliberately looks at boring regulated jobs.
- **Unsexy filter.** Ideas that look unglamorous are under-explored for the
  same reason.

## Limits

The essay assumes founders who can do sales, raise money and talk to users
in person. For a founder whose `FOUNDER.md` rules those out, "a few people
who want it a lot" still has to pass gates 2 and 5: the few must be
reachable self-serve.
