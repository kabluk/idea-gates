# Clayton Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon, David Duncan — *Competing Against Luck* (2016)

The book-length statement of jobs-to-be-done theory.

## Ideas we use

- **Customers hire a product to make progress in a circumstance.** The unit
  of analysis is the job (situation + desired progress), not the customer's
  demographics or the product's features. → Every card names the job as
  "when <situation>, they want to <progress>".
- **The real competition is whatever they hire today**, including
  spreadsheets, a cousin, doing nothing. → Every card names the substitute;
  gate 3 prices against it, and "nothing" as a substitute is a warning that
  the job may not hurt enough.
- **Jobs have functional, social and emotional sides.** People pay to
  avoid looking incompetent or anxious as much as to save time. → In
  compliance-type niches the emotional job (fear of a fine) often carries
  the price.
- **Look for non-consumption and workarounds.** People who cobble something
  together, or who give up, show a job that is badly served.
  → Workarounds in forums and reviews are first-class evidence.
- **Firing.** Understanding what makes customers stop using the current
  solution matters as much as what makes them start.

## Limits

The book's cases are large companies with research budgets for customer
interviews. The scout cannot interview; it infers the job from public traces
(reviews, forum posts, queries), which is weaker evidence and is marked as
such.
