# Rob Fitzpatrick — *The Mom Test* (2013)

How to learn from customer conversations when everyone is inclined to be
polite.

## Ideas we use

- **Opinions about the future are worthless; past behaviour is evidence.**
  "Would you pay for…" gets a kind yes. "When did this last happen, what did
  you do, what did it cost you?" gets data. → Evidence lines quote what
  people did or paid, not what they say they would like.
- **Compliments and hypothetical interest are false positives.**
  → An upvoted "great idea!" thread is not demand.
- **Commitment is the signal**: time, reputation or money given up front.
  → Paid workarounds and paid inferior tools are the strongest public
  evidence the scout can find.
- **Talk about their life, not your idea.** → The scout reads where people
  describe their problems unprompted, not launch threads where they react
  to a pitch.

## Limits

The book is about conversations, which this founder profile may rule out.
We use its evidence hierarchy for public traces: paid behaviour > described
behaviour > stated interest > compliments.
