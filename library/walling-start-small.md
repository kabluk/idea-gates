# Rob Walling — *Start Small, Stay Small: A Developer's Guide to Launching a Startup* (2010)

The case for small, self-funded software businesses run by one developer.

## Ideas we use

- **Market first, product second.** Find a niche with demonstrated demand
  before writing code. → Matches the gates-before-code order; the ocean
  phase is where demand is measured.
- **Small niches are a feature for a solo founder**: too small to attract
  funded competitors, big enough for one person's income.
  → The judge's "honest ceiling" is not a weakness to apologise for.
- **Search-driven niches** suit founders who will not sell: if people
  already search for the problem, a product can be found without outreach.
  → Search volume for tool or purchase intent is the preferred evidence
  for gate 5 when the founder's only channel is organic search.
- **Prefer products you can market without being present**: content,
  SEO, integrations, marketplaces of an existing platform.
- **Time is the scarcest resource of a part-time founder** — favour ideas
  with low support load and no custom work per customer.

## Limits

Written before modern app marketplaces and AI tooling shifted costs; the
specific tactics date, the constraints-first reasoning does not.
