## Telegram (RU)

**Где брать идеи для SaaS: у компаний, которые только что резко подняли цены**

Посмотрел кейс Алмаса Абулхаирова: он навайбкодил замену Harvest и дошёл до $346 MRR без рекламы. Повод — новый владелец Harvest, Bending Spoons, перевёл клиентов на тариф с доплатами за использование; по словам автора — в 5–20 раз; BBC приводила пример, где счёт вырос с $130 до $2 110 в месяц.

Как устроен его плейбук:
1. Злые клиенты на Reddit → замена по старой цене конкурента ($11 за место)
2. Бесплатные каналы: посты на Reddit (в сабреддите Harvest за это не банят), SEO-статьи, профиль на G2
3. Каждому новому пользователю — личное сообщение в течение нескольких часов; крупным — продлённый триал в обмен на созвон
4. Главный урок: продавал «красивые счета», а платят за учёт времени команды. Спрашивайте платящих

Где споткнётесь: реклама в ChatGPT показывается только на тарифах Free и Go — платящие B2B-клиенты её не видят. А таргет Reddit Ads на маленький сабреддит, по опыту автора, не держится.

Что мне кажется самым интересным: волна Harvest уже поздняя, а Bending Spoons объявил о покупке Airtable и Miro. Если схема повторится — следующая волна злых клиентов впереди.

## X thread (EN)

1/ A vibe-coded Harvest alternative is at $346 MRR with zero ad spend. The idea source is the interesting part: customers of a product whose new owner just multiplied its prices.

2/ Context: after Bending Spoons bought Harvest, it moved to per-seat plus usage fees. The BBC reported a monthly bill going from $130 to $2,110. Per the author, long-time users posted "goodbye after 15 years" threads on Reddit.

3/ The playbook (from Almas Abulkhairov's video): build the replacement at the competitor's old price ($11/seat), post on Reddit (the Harvest subreddit doesn't ban it), write SEO pages for "<competitor> alternative", set up a G2 profile.

4/ The part most founders skip: a personal message to every new signup within hours. Bigger accounts got an extended trial in exchange for a call — teams of 10+ seats now pay $100+/month.

5/ His biggest lesson: he positioned it as invoicing, but customers pay for team time tracking. Ask the people who pay, not everyone.

6/ Two ad notes: ChatGPT ads only reach Free and Go users, so paying B2B buyers on Plus/Business won't see them. And Reddit Ads targeting a subreddit with ~1,000 weekly visitors produced ~150K impressions — the targeting didn't hold.

7/ My take: the Harvest wave is late, but Bending Spoons has announced deals for Airtable and Miro. If the pattern repeats, the next wave of angry customers is ahead.

## LinkedIn (EN)

Some of the best SaaS ideas start with someone else's pricing decision.

I watched a founder walk through his vibe-coded alternative to Harvest. After Bending Spoons acquired Harvest and moved customers to usage-based fees, long-time users started looking for a way out. He built a replacement at Harvest's old per-seat price and reached $346 MRR with no ad spend.

How it works:
→ Find the angry customers (Reddit threads about the price change)
→ Build the core job, priced at the old price, with no usage surcharges
→ Free channels: Reddit where allowed, SEO pages, a G2 profile
→ A personal message to every new signup; extended trials for teams in exchange for a call

What to watch for: ChatGPT ads only reach Free and Go users, so paying business buyers won't see them.

The lesson I'm taking: talk to the customers who pay. He thought he sold invoicing; they were paying for time tracking.

Which tool you rely on would you replace tomorrow if its price went up 10x?
